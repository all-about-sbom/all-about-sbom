---

One of the key use cases for SBOMs is to provide transparency to your customers about the software you are shipping.
By providing an SBOM, you can give your customers confidence in your product and your development practices.

This is especially important for customers in regulated industries, such as healthcare and finance, where there are strict requirements for software supply chain security.

To learn more about how to generate and provide SBOMs to your customers, please see the [Producer section](/roles/producer) of the SBOM lifecycle.

Both the European Union's Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) and Executive Order 14028 in the United States have identified the software supply chain as critical to securing any digital product.
Beyond these legislative examples, customers or partners may also want to perform their own due diligence on the software they intend to deploy.
Thus, a vendor needs to be able to provide documentation on the software composition of their product.
SBOMs are the tool to do so and are even explicitly mentioned by the CRA.

##### Example

A newly developed software product is to be released on the European market.
To comply with regulations, the software supply chain has to be documented.
The CRA explicitly names SBOMs as the solution to this.
