## What is an SBOM?

A Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) is a structured, machine‑readable inventory of the components, libraries, and dependencies inside a software product.  
Think of it like a food label: it tells you what went into the software, which versions are used, and where each component comes from.

At minimum, an SBOM typically includes:

- Product name and version
- List of components and their versions
- Supplier or source of each component
- Unique identifiers (e.g., PURL, CPE) and dependency relationships
- Timestamp and author of the SBOM data

Common formats such as **CycloneDX** and **SPDX** make SBOMs easy to process automatically in tooling and pipelines.

## Why SBOMs matter

Modern applications are assembled from many third‑party components, creating complex software supply chains.  
When a new vulnerability is disclosed, teams often struggle to answer a simple question: *“Are we affected, and where?”*.

SBOMs create the transparency needed to:
- **Find vulnerable components quickly** and prioritize remediation.
- **Support compliance** with customer, sector, and regulatory expectations.
- **Manage technical debt** by detecting outdated or unsupported components.
- **Increase supplier transparency** by sharing software composition details with customers and partners.

This website helps you understand SBOMs and start using them in your own projects or organization.
